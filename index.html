On Being Someone's Constant

Some things don't change: distance, time zones, or the fact that I'm not going anywhere.

Listen, I need to tell you something, and I'm going to dress it up in Times New Roman and call it an article because apparently that's easier than just saying it directly.

You know how people always say "I'm here for you"? It's become this thing we say automatically, like "how are you" when we don't actually want to know how someone is. It's a reflex. A nicety.

But I mean it literally. I am here. Not just in the "I'll be here if you need me" conditional way. I'm here regardless of whether you need me, whether you want me, whether things are good or bad, whether you're handling everything perfectly or falling apart.

I'm here the way gravity is here. You don't have to think about it or check if it's still working. It just is.

The Distance Thing

We're 2,083 kilometers apart. I know this because I googled it once and the number stuck in my head like an annoying song lyric. That's far enough that we can't just meet for coffee when one of us is having a bad day. It's far enough that time zones matter. It's far enough that "see you soon" requires plane tickets and planning.

But here's what I've learned about distance: it's terrible for logistics and completely irrelevant for everything else.

"Geography is just geography. It doesn't change the fact that when you're stressed about exams, I'm thinking about you."

You could be on another continent and I'd still be here. You could move to Antarctica to study penguins and I'd still be here. The physical space between us has exactly zero correlation with how present I am in this.

When you're happy, I want to hear about it in real time, not the sanitized version you'd tell someone else. When you're upset, I want the raw, unfiltered truth.

The Anniversary, Delayed

We hit our first anniversary on the nineteenth—exactly one year since we spoke for the very first time. I wanted to give you this then, but work and life and the reality of coordinates got in the way, so you are reading this on the twenty-first. In a way, though, that delay is the entire point.

Love isn't a museum piece that has to be dusted off and celebrated exactly on the stroke of midnight or else it loses its value. It exists in the messy gaps between the nineteenth and the twenty-first, in the busy hours, in the moments when we are tired but still looking in the same direction. It is robust enough to survive work schedules, timezone lags, and the general friction of being alive. Celebrating late doesn't mean we missed it; it means we are building something that doesn't depend on perfect timing to survive.

The Unconditional Part

Most support comes with invisible conditions. "I'm here for you" often means "I'm here for you if you're struggling" or "I'm here for you if you need me" or "I'm here for you unless you push me away."

That's not this.

I'm here for you when you're doing great. I'm here for you when you're doing terribly. I'm here for you when you want to talk and when you need space. I'm here for you when you're easy to love and when you're being difficult. I'm here for you on days when you feel like you're too much and on days when you feel like you're not enough.

The conditions don't exist. I've looked for them. They're not there.

What This Actually Means

Okay, practically speaking, what does "I'm always here for you" actually mean? Because it's easy to say and hard to define.

It means if you text me at 3 a.m. because you can't sleep, I'm reading it. Even if I'm asleep, I'm reading it when I wake up, and it matters.

It means if something good happens and I'm the person you want to tell, that means everything to me.

It means if you fail an exam or don't get something you wanted or have a day where everything feels heavy, I don't need you to pretend it's fine. You can just say it's not fine, and I'm not going anywhere.

It means you don't have to earn this. You don't have to be perfect or impressive or easy. You just have to be you, and that's been enough since day one.

The Appreciation Part

I should probably also mention that I think you're extraordinary, but you get weird when I say that too directly, so I'll bury it in the middle of an article where maybe you won't notice.

You're a poet who loves biology. You write about emotions with precision and study cells with curiosity. Most people have to choose between art and science, between feeling deeply and thinking clearly. You just refused to choose, and somehow that works.

You feel things intensely. This sometimes makes things harder for you, but it's also what makes you interesting. Shallow people are easy to be around and boring to know. You're the opposite.

And you're beautiful, which I'm contractually obligated to mention because it's true and also because you'll accuse me of not mentioning it if I don't.

The Reassurance Part

I know you get in your head sometimes. I know you worry about things that probably won't happen. I know you're sometimes convinced you're too much or not enough or that people will eventually get tired of you.

So let me be extremely clear: I'm not going anywhere.

I'm not going anywhere if you don't crack NEET. I'm not going anywhere if you do crack NEET. I'm not going anywhere if you end up on a completely different path than you planned.

Your worth to me was never conditional on your achievements. It's conditional on nothing. You're just worth it, and that's the whole equation.

The "But What About" Section

I can hear you coming up with exceptions in your head. "But what if I..." and "But what about when..."

So let's address those:

What if you're annoying? You're not, but even if you were, I'm here.

What if you need time alone? Take it. I'll be here when you're done.

What if you're convinced I'll eventually leave? I won't. Eventually is a long time, and I'll still be here.

The Part Where I Ruin the Seriousness

Look, I know this whole thing is very sincere and might be making you uncomfortable, so let me ruin it by saying something stupid:

I've memorized how you type. I can tell when you're tired because your messages get longer and more stream-of-consciousness. I know when you're stressed because you suddenly become extremely formal. I know when you're happy because your texts get shorter and you actually use punctuation less.

Also, you send voice notes at the most random times, and they take up 60% of my phone storage, and I've never once considered deleting them because apparently I'm that person now.

The Actual Point

What I'm trying to say, buried under all these words, is simple:

You have someone who's not leaving. Not conditionally. Not temporarily. Not unless things are easy.

You have someone who's 2,083 kilometers away and completely present anyway.

You have me. Permanently. Whether you need it right now or not.

I'm here. I'm staying here. That's not changing.

The End (Sort Of)

This is the part where I'm supposed to wrap this up with something profound, but honestly, I've said what I needed to say.

You're appreciated. You're valued. You're not alone in this, whatever "this" is.

And if you don't believe me, I put some puzzles below that prove it. They solve themselves, which feels appropriate because you also solve yourself—you figure things out, you work through things, you're more capable than you give yourself credit for.

But also, I'm here. Just in case you need a reminder.

Always.

—Niv
