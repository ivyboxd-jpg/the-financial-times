The Phonetics of Fall

I am a creature of words, usually. I like to think I can engineer a sentence to do exactly what I want. But then she typed my name—just those three letters—and followed it with a question mark. "niv?" It was such a small thing, but it felt like being tapped on the shoulder by fate. In that second, the internal monologue stopped.

There is a specific kind of hunger that develops when you hear a person’s voice through a screen for long enough. You start to crave the acoustics of it. I realized then that I didn't just want to read my name in her font; I wanted to hear it drift across a hallway. I wanted to hear her say it when she’s annoyed, when she’s laughing, and when she’s half-asleep. I decided, quite suddenly, that I wanted to be "Niv" to her for the rest of my life.

this was the exact moment i was finished.

The Contract

Some people flirt with eye contact; Aaradhya flirts with curated frequencies. She’s the one who sent the Blend. She didn’t ask permission to enter my ears; she just dropped the link like a gauntlet. And then, as if the invitation wasn't enough, she followed it up with the four words that basically signed my life away: "stamped and sealed."

It’s a terrifyingly confident thing to say. It sounds like a treaty. It sounds final. I sat there looking at my phone, thinking about how I was supposed to act normal when this girl was out here "stamping and sealing" our shared taste in music before we’d even had a proper first date. I didn't stand a chance. I still don't.

she really just decided for us, didn't she?

The Wooden Box

Infinite Architecture

Then there was the talk of the wooden box. She told me she keeps her love letters there. It’s such a tangible, romantic image—a physical home for fleeting thoughts. I tried to play it cool, telling her to "nestle" my messages in there. I tried to sound like I knew what I was doing. But then the panic hit, that classic *oh-god-i'm-being-too-much* feeling, and I scrambled to take it back. "Just kidding by the way I’m too bad at this."

She didn't let me get away with it. She called me a liar. She saw the poet in me even when I was trying to hide him. And then she said that line about memories—that space is abundant. That there is always more room to fill. It’s a beautiful way to look at a relationship; not as a container that gets full, but as an architecture that keeps expanding. Eleven months in, and we’re still building rooms.
