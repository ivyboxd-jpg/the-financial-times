01
Orientation

Mission Briefing

In Project Hail Mary, Ryland Grace wakes up on a spaceship millions of miles from Earth. He has no memory, dead crewmates, and an extinction-level threat to solve. He figures it out anyway. He uses the scientific method. He ships the solution via the Beetles. He saves the world.

You are not expected to save the world this weekend. But you are expected to show up, figure it out with your crew, and ship something real before the window closes.

If you are reading this and thinking "I don't belong here, everyone else knows more than me" — that feeling is normal. It is also wrong. Every person registered for this mission felt the same thing. The ones who survive are the ones who show up anyway.

Hack & Ship is beginner-friendly by design. You do not need to have shipped a product before. You do not need to code at a professional level. You need a problem worth solving, a crew willing to work, and enough honesty to say when you're stuck.

03
Team Structure

Crew & Alliances

Your team has been matched for you. You did not choose them. Grace didn't choose Rocky. Rocky didn't choose Grace. They showed up, built a common language, and engineered a solution neither could have built alone. That's the objective.

Assign Roles on Day One

In the first 30 minutes, decide who owns what. These don't have to be permanent, but they must exist.

Problem Lead // THE GRACE
Owns the problem definition. Ensures the crew doesn't drift from the actual hypothesis they are testing.
Build Lead // THE ROCKY
Owns the technical approach. Makes the hard calls when the team is stuck on implementation or materials.
Pitch Lead // COMMUNICATOR
Owns the presentation from hour one. The story and data logs are built alongside the product, not after.
Timekeeper // MISSION CLOCK
Watches the Petrova line. Calls check-ins every hour. Flags when the team is stuck in a resource sink.

The Hourly Sync (🎵 Question? Answer! 🎵)

Once an hour, every crew member answers three questions out loud:

  • What did I just finish?
  • What am I working on next?
  • Is anything blocking my progress?

The most critical feature gets built first. Once your core idea works — even badly — you have something to put in the Beetle. Decide on your critical path in the first hour and protect it.

Apr 17 Evening → Apr 18 Morning

Phase 1: The Hypothesis

The proposal is not just a pitch deck. It's a clarity exercise. Crews that pass the selection gate are the ones who clearly understand the anomaly they are solving and why their approach makes scientific sense.

What a good hypothesis answers:

01
Who specifically faces this anomaly?
Not "everyone." A real demographic in a specific situation.
02
What is the actual cost?
Time, resources, extinction? Make it concrete. If you can't measure the cost, you don't understand it.
03
What are you engineering, and how does it solve it?
One sentence. If it takes a paragraph, the idea is too complex. Simplify.
04
What is the simplest working prototype?
Not the final vision. The smallest piece of tech that proves the science works.
05
Why this crew? Why now?
What unique perspective do you bring to this problem? Use your reality.

The most common failure mode is falling in love with a technical solution before understanding the problem. Judges evaluate problem definition first. A brilliant solution to a vague problem loses to a simple solution to a well-understood one.

05
Apr 18 Afternoon → Apr 19 Evening

Phase 2: Engineering the Solution

You made it through selection. Now you build. The rules are simple: scope small, start immediately, and load the Beetles before the window closes.

Prototype, not product. You are not building the final Hail Mary. You are building the small-scale model that proves the physics work. One feature. Working. Submittable.

Energy Management

  • Protect your sleep. Decisions made on zero rest are expensive.
  • Take a real break every 90 minutes. Step away from the monitors.
  • Eat before you realize you're hungry. You will forget while in flow.
  • If a system has been broken for 30 minutes, ping Mission Control.

The Pitch runs parallel to the Build

Your Pitch Lead is building the story while the code is written. By the time the prototype is ready, the presentation should be 80% done. Tell it in five minutes. One person presents. Record your demo — live technology at T-Zero is historically unreliable.

06
Support Network

Mission Control

Mission Control (Stratt) didn't build the ship for Grace. They gave him the resources to figure it out himself. That is your Mentor.

  • Contact them early. A mentor looped in at hour one is infinitely more useful than one pinged at hour twenty.
  • State the blockage, not the requested answer. "We can't decide between approaches A and B" is better than "Which do we pick?"
  • The 30-Minute Rule. Stuck for 30 minutes? Stop and ask for help. Silence is the enemy of survival.

Comms Channels (Discord)

All support runs through Discord. Know your frequencies:

  • #mission-briefing — Strat-level announcements, schedule updates.
  • #ground-control — Logistics and organizer questions.
  • #launch-pad — Crew chatter, morale, progress sharing.
  • #help — Stuck? Ping here. Someone is awake.

08
Endgame

Evaluation Criteria

Judges are not evaluating the complexity of your code. They are evaluating if you understood a real problem and engineered a prototype that addresses it.

01
Problem Definition
Is the anomaly real? Is it specific? Do you clearly understand who faces it and the cost? Evaluated first.
02
Approach & Hypothesis
Does the solution address the anomaly? Did you make thoughtful engineering tradeoffs given your time constraints?
03
Execution (The Build)
Does it work? Even partially. A single working feature scores exponentially higher than an ambitious but broken shell.
04
Presentation & Comms
Can you brief command in five minutes? You are judged on what you communicate, not on what is hidden in your code repository.

You are judged on 5 minutes of presentation, not on 36 hours of engineering. Teams fail when they build something incredible and fail to explain why it matters. The pitch matters as much as the build. Amaze!
