On being a poet, being a muse, and the terrifying realization that some people can change your life in exactly four characters.

I. THE SYLLABLES

I didn't think I was a particularly easy person to pin down. I liked my autonomy, my quiet, my carefully curated distance. But then she typed three letters and a punctuation mark, and the gravity in the room shifted so violently I almost felt the floor tilt. "niv?"

It’s a strange thing, hearing your name from someone you’re just beginning to know. It’s even stranger when you realize, in the split second of reading it, that you’ve developed a new life goal: I want to hear her say my name from across a crowded room, from the kitchen while I'm in the hallway, from the pillow next to mine—every single day for the rest of my life.

this was the exact moment i was finished.

II. STAMPED AND SEALED

Then she sent the Blend. She didn't ask if I liked her taste; she didn't apologize for whatever weird indie tracks might pop up. She just dropped the link and waited. It felt like a test I didn't want to fail, a digital handshake that carried the weight of a blood oath.

I joined. I saw our music collide. I told her it was stamped and sealed. It was a joke, mostly—a bit of dramatic flair—but underneath the humor, I was signing a contract I had no intention of ever canceling.

she's the one who started it. don't let her tell you otherwise.

III. THE WOODEN BOX

We talk a lot about poetry, but the most poetic thing she ever said to me wasn't in a verse. It was about a wooden box. She told me she keeps her love letters there. I tried to be smooth—I tried to tell her to "nestle" this one in there, promethean and confident. And then, because I am human and because she makes me nervous in the best way, I immediately panicked. "Just kidding by the way I’m too bad at this."

She called me a liar. She saw right through the self-deprecation. She told me I was good at this, and then she said it again: oddly comforting. She has this way of finding the exact word for a feeling I didn't even know I was projecting. The idea that space for memories is infinite—that the box will never truly be full because we’ll just keep building bigger ones.
