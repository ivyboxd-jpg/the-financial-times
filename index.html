On loving a poet from 2,083 kilometers away, and the terrifying beauty of three characters in a chat bubble.

I want to tell you something I've never said out loud, which is that I knew on the very first day. It wasn’t a cinematic epiphany—no violins, no slow-motion rain. It was quieter. It was the specific, nagging weight of a phone in my palm, and the realization that I had turned on Instagram notifications for the first time in my life just to see if a stranger would reply to a story that no longer exists.

But the real shift happened later. It was a Tuesday evening when she sent three letters and a question mark: "niv?"

Most people hear their name and think of identity. I heard it and thought of a destination. When I saw it in that purple bubble, I knew I wanted to hear it across a room every single morning for the rest of my life. I wanted to hear it in her voice, flavored by her moods, echoing in a shared hallway. It wasn't just my name anymore; it was an invitation I wasn't prepared to refuse.

II. STAMPED AND SEALED

Memory is a messy thing, but music is precise. She sent me the Blend link. No warning, just a digital handshake made of frequencies and hidden meanings. It was the kind of intimacy that bypasses small talk—here is what I listen to when I'm alone; do you belong in this space too?

I joined immediately. And then she said it: "stamped and sealed." It sounded like a legal decree, like she was notarizing our shared reality before we’d even had the chance to define it. It was humorous, authoritative, and completely terrifying. I realized then that I wasn't just talking to a girl; I was entering a contract with a force of nature.

III. THE WOODEN BOX

The distance between us is exactly 2,083 kilometers, but when we talk about the wooden box, it feels like we’re in the same room. She told me she keeps her love letters there. I tried to play the role of the smooth poet—I told her to "nestle" my messages in there, acting like I had it all figured out. Then, the inevitable panic of sincerity hit, and I tried to take it back: "Just kidding by the way i'm too bad at this."

She called me a liar. She saw the effort and the heart and she refused to let me hide behind a joke. She said it was "oddly comforting" that space for memories is infinite. That we’ll never run out of room because we’ll just build more boxes. And then she said the words that make my heart skip: "watch me write a poem again."
